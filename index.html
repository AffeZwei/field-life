Mass per color exact

Every color is a density field on the grid instead of a swarm of dots. Each step, each field is blurred at two radii; the difference of the two blurs is a ring-shaped neighbourhood integral — the field version of “how much of that color sits about R away from here.” Those rings are mixed by the interaction matrix into one affinity map per color, minus a short-range term that makes everything crowd-shy. Then MaCE moves the mass: every cell hands its own mass to its 9 neighbours in proportion to exp(β·affinity), so nothing is ever created or destroyed — the totals on the left stay put forever, and the patterns can neither explode nor die out.
